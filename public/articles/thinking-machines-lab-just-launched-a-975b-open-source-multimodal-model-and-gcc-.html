Thinking Machines Lab Just Launched a 975B Open-Source Multimodal Model — And GCC Marketing Teams Have a New Frontier Alternative to Factor Into Every Pipeline Decision

Inkling, a 975-billion-parameter open-source model trained on video and audio, expands the frontier model vendor landscape and reshapes the build-vs-subscribe calculus for MENA marketing and content operations.
July 19, 2026·6 min read·Conneqt

A new entrant at frontier scale has arrived. Thinking Machines Lab has released Inkling, a 975-billion-parameter open-source multimodal model trained to understand both video and audio. The release, reported by Wired, positions Thinking Machines as a direct competitor to closed-model providers such as Anthropic and OpenAI — companies that have, until recently, defined the ceiling of what enterprise AI teams could reach for.

What Makes This a Launch-Class Event

Parameter count alone does not make a model useful, and this publication has no independent benchmark data on Inkling's real-world performance. What matters strategically is the combination of three attributes arriving together: scale (975 billion parameters places Inkling in the same weight class as the largest frontier models), modality breadth (video and audio comprehension, not just text), and open-source licensing. Each of those attributes is significant individually. Together, they constitute a meaningful shift in what operators can self-host or fine-tune without negotiating a commercial API agreement.

The open-source designation is the detail GCC technology teams should examine most carefully — specifically what "open source" means in practice for a model of this size. Licensing terms, compute requirements for inference, and any use-case restrictions will determine whether Inkling is genuinely deployable by a regional team or whether it remains, practically speaking, a research artifact that runs only on hyperscaler-grade infrastructure.

Why Multimodal at This Scale Changes the Vendor Conversation

Most of the frontier model conversation in MENA enterprise circles has centered on text: Arabic language quality, dialect handling, retrieval-augmented generation for compliance-heavy sectors. Multimodal capability — specifically video and audio — has been available primarily through closed commercial APIs, which creates a dependency that some regional operators, particularly those in financial services and government-adjacent verticals, have treated with increasing caution.

A 975-billion-parameter model that natively processes video and audio opens a different class of use case. For GCC marketing and content teams specifically, the implication is that tasks currently requiring a stitched pipeline — separate transcription, translation, summarization, and visual understanding tools — could in principle collapse into a single model call. That consolidation matters for latency, cost structure, and the number of third-party data-sharing agreements an operator needs to maintain.

Whether Inkling actually delivers on that consolidation promise is an empirical question that vendor documentation and early adopter reporting will need to answer. The point is that a credible open-source option at this modality and parameter level now exists on the market, where six months ago it did not.

The Build-vs-Subscribe Calculus Is Getting More Complex

For the past two years, the practical answer for most MENA enterprises evaluating frontier AI has been: subscribe to a commercial API, accept the data residency trade-offs, and move fast. The open-source alternatives available at the high end — capable of handling multilingual multimedia workloads at production quality — were limited enough that the subscribe path was almost always the faster route to capability.

Inkling's arrival does not reverse that calculus overnight. Inference costs for a nearly trillion-parameter model are not trivial, and regional cloud infrastructure capable of serving it efficiently is not uniformly available. But it does mean that the next time a GCC content operation, broadcaster, or performance marketing team evaluates its AI stack, the vendor comparison set is larger than it was. Thinking Machines Lab is now a name that belongs in that evaluation alongside the established closed-model providers.

The competitive pressure this creates is also worth noting. Anthropic and OpenAI do not lose enterprise accounts immediately because an open-source competitor launches, but sustained open-source progress at frontier scale disciplines pricing and accelerates feature development across the board. For buyers, that is a favorable dynamic regardless of which model they ultimately run.

What Regional Teams Should Do Now

The immediate action is not adoption — it is monitoring. Teams responsible for AI vendor strategy in GCC marketing and media operations should track Inkling's licensing documentation, its inference requirements, and the first wave of independent benchmarks on Arabic-language and multimedia performance specifically. Open-source models have historically varied widely in non-English language quality, and that gap is the single most important variable for MENA deployment decisions.

Thinking Machines Lab is a new name in a market where trust is built through performance data, not press releases. The 975-billion-parameter open-source multimodal launch earns attention. Whether it earns adoption will depend on what independent evaluation finds.
