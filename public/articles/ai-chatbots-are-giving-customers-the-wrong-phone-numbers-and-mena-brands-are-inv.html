AI Chatbots Are Giving Customers the Wrong Phone Numbers — And MENA Brands Are Invisible to the Problem

AI-generated search answers are routing real customer calls to wrong numbers at scale, and for MENA brands investing in local search optimisation, the reputational and conversion damage may already be happening undetected.
May 17, 2026·6 min read·Conneqt

A Redditor recently described being "desperate for help" after roughly a month of his personal phone being flooded with calls from strangers seeking a lawyer, a product designer, and a locksmith. The calls were not random. According to reporting by MIT Technology Review, the callers had been misdirected by Google's generative AI — which had apparently surfaced his number in place of the businesses they were actually trying to reach. Separately, a software developer in Israel reported being contacted on WhatsApp by people looking for services he had no connection to, through the same mechanism.

These are not edge cases in a beta product. Google's AI Overviews — the generative answer panels now appearing at the top of search results globally — are pulling contact information and presenting it as authoritative. When that information is wrong, the failure is silent from the brand's perspective. The business never sees the misdirected call. The customer, frustrated and unable to reach anyone useful, simply moves on.

Why Contact Data Breaks Inside AI-Generated Answers

Traditional search surfaces links, which users click and verify. Generative AI surfaces answers, which users act on directly. The accountability gap between those two experiences is significant. A wrong phone number buried in a directory listing creates friction but is correctable. A wrong phone number read aloud by a voice assistant or displayed in a prominent AI Overview panel gets treated as ground truth.

The underlying problem is that large language models and retrieval-augmented systems are trained on and indexed against web data that is frequently outdated, duplicated, or conflicting. Business phone numbers change. Owners sell. Numbers get reassigned by telecoms. Structured data on Google Business Profiles may be accurate, but AI systems may be drawing on older cached pages, third-party directories, or scraped social profiles that reflect a previous state of the world. The model has no reliable way to adjudicate between these sources — and often does not flag the uncertainty to the user at all.

The MENA Exposure Is Structural, Not Incidental

For operators across the GCC and broader MENA region, this is not a theoretical risk. Several structural features of the market amplify it.

First, the regional business directory ecosystem is fragmented. Businesses frequently appear across multiple local listing platforms, social media accounts, and aggregator sites — many of which are inconsistently maintained and rarely audited. The gap between what a brand controls on its own Google Business Profile and what AI systems actually ingest when generating an answer can be substantial.

Second, WhatsApp is a primary customer service channel across KSA, UAE, Egypt, and other markets in ways that have no real parallel in Western markets. A misdirected WhatsApp contact is not a minor nuisance — it is a broken service interaction in the channel where customers most expect to be heard. The Israeli developer's experience, cited in the MIT Technology Review report, is directly analogous to what MENA business owners face when their WhatsApp Business numbers are incorrectly indexed or conflated with personal numbers in AI outputs.

Third, many regional brands — particularly in retail, hospitality, and financial services — have invested materially in Google AI Overviews optimisation as a growth lever. The implication is that optimisation efforts focused purely on appearing inside AI-generated answers, without equal attention to the accuracy of contact data within those answers, may be generating visibility that actively misdirects customers.

What an Audit Actually Requires

The MIT Technology Review reporting does not prescribe a fix, and there is no complete one available. Google has not published a reliable mechanism for brands to verify how their contact information appears inside AI-generated responses, as distinct from standard search results. That gap itself warrants attention from regional marketing and CX leadership.

In practice, a minimum audit involves querying Google with the kinds of natural-language prompts customers actually use — "phone number for [brand] in Riyadh," "contact [brand] customer service," "WhatsApp for [brand]" — and recording what the AI surfaces. It means cross-referencing that output against every directory, aggregator, and social profile where the brand's number appears. And it means treating contact data hygiene not as a one-time setup task but as a recurring operational control, given that AI systems are continuously re-indexing the web.

The broader pattern here is one that will recur as generative AI embeds deeper into search, maps, and voice interfaces. AI systems are extraordinarily confident in their outputs and structurally poor at surfacing their own uncertainty. For MENA brands, the cost of that confidence — when it is misplaced — lands on the customer, invisibly, and on the brand's conversion rate, silently.
