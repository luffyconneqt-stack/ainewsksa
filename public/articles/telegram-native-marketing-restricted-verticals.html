The Telegram-native marketing playbook every restricted vertical is copying

When Meta and Google won't run your ads, you stop renting attention and build owned funnels. 1xBet pioneered it. Crypto projects, supplements, and dating verticals are now running variants of the same architecture.
May 7, 2026·5 min read·Conneqt

Most marketers learned in 2023 and 2024 what gambling and crypto operators figured out years earlier: paid social is rented attention. Meta and Google can shut your ad account tomorrow with no recourse, and several verticals can't run there at all. The operators who scaled fastest in restricted verticals didn't fight harder for blocked creatives. They moved the funnel onto a platform they could own.

Telegram became that platform.

The architecture

The pattern, mapped publicly across 1xBet, several crypto projects, and increasingly dating and supplement verticals, runs roughly as follows. UGC creators, KOLs, affiliate channels and forum posts seed entry links — all pointing to a single Telegram bot rather than a landing page. The bot greets users with a welcome message and bonus offer framed as exclusive to the TG channel. A 2–3 message conversational flow captures soft KYC: country, language, age confirmation. The bot pushes deposit with limited-time framing using inline buttons that handle the redirect in a single tap. A starter conversion is suggested matched to user interest. Triggered messages handle reactivation on inactivity windows. Cross-sell happens in the same thread.

The bot is the funnel. The Telegram channels surrounding it (main, announcements, community, regional offshoots) are the broadcast and retention layer keeping the audience warm between conversion events.

Why it works structurally

Four properties make the architecture defensible.

No domain to ban. Telegram is the platform. A regulator banning a landing page can't ban Telegram in most markets. A blocked creative on Meta is a Tuesday inconvenience.

Same-thread conversion. Web2 funnels lose 60–80% of clicks at the landing page through load times, signup friction, and redirects. The TG funnel keeps users inside one conversation from first message to first deposit.

Permanent push channel. Once a user messages the bot, they're a permanent push surface. No re-acquisition cost; reactivation is one message away.

Cross-vertical leverage. The same architecture works for sportsbook, crypto, casino, supplements, sweepstakes, and dating. The payload changes; the spine doesn't.

Where the model breaks

The architecture's failure mode is content volume. A live TG funnel demands daily channel posts, regional language variants, alpha-on-trigger automations, paid TG channel ad creative, KOL drop scripts, and bot conversation trees. A serious crypto project running this funnel ships 30+ TG posts per day across channels and bot replies. Most operators stall here — they can design the funnel but can't fill it.

This is where AI content engines have started compounding. The funnel architecture is increasingly common knowledge; the differentiator has moved to the operators who can ship the content at the cadence and language coverage the funnel actually needs to scale.

Implications for compliance-friendly verticals

The interesting question is whether the architecture leaks back into compliance-friendly verticals. Several B2B SaaS companies have started running variants of it — top-of-funnel content drives users into a Telegram or Discord bot rather than a sales-funnel landing page. The conversion mechanics are similar enough that the model is portable.

For marketers operating in restricted verticals, the question isn't whether to adopt the architecture — that's settled. The question is which content layer scales it without burning out the channel. The funnel pattern has been public for years. The operators ahead are the ones who've solved content volume, not architecture.
