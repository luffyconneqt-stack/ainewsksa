Beyond ChatGPT: Why NotebookLM Is Becoming the Agency Tool MENA Teams Are Sleeping On

Google's NotebookLM offers agency teams a source-grounded AI research environment that most GCC shops have yet to explore — here are seven workflow use cases worth knowing about.
May 15, 2026·6 min read·Conneqt

Most agency conversations about AI in the GCC begin and end with ChatGPT. That is understandable — it is accessible, well-marketed, and usable without onboarding. But it is also increasingly insufficient for the kind of structured, source-specific analytical work that agencies actually need to do: competitive audits, client briefings, strategy synthesis, media research. For those tasks, Google's NotebookLM is worth a serious look — not as a replacement for general-purpose large language models, but as a distinct instrument with a different set of strengths.

What NotebookLM Actually Does

NotebookLM, currently available free through Google Labs, operates as a closed research environment. Users upload their own source documents — PDFs, Google Docs, web links, YouTube transcripts — and the model answers questions, generates summaries, and synthesizes insights exclusively from those materials. It does not draw on its general training data when responding. That constraint, which sounds like a limitation, is in practice its main advantage: outputs are traceable, citations are specific, and hallucinations rooted in invented sources are structurally harder to produce. For agency work that depends on client confidentiality and defensible analysis, that matters.

Seven Use Cases With Practical Weight

1. Competitive landscape synthesis. Upload a set of competitor annual reports, press releases, and earned media coverage, then query across all of them simultaneously. NotebookLM can identify positioning patterns, messaging gaps, and product claims that would take an analyst hours to extract manually. For KSA agencies supporting Vision 2030-adjacent clients in sectors like fintech, tourism, or logistics, where the competitive field is shifting rapidly, this compresses research cycles meaningfully.

2. Client briefing preparation. Before onboarding a new client, load their existing content, previous campaign reports, any available market research, and category news. Ask NotebookLM to produce a structured briefing document. The output is grounded in the actual source material rather than generic AI assumptions about the industry — a distinction that becomes obvious when the client reads it.

3. RFP and pitch research. Agencies responding to public-sector tenders in Saudi Arabia or the UAE increasingly need to demonstrate category expertise quickly. NotebookLM can process procurement documents, prior tender awards, and sector reports to surface the specific language and priorities that matter to evaluators.

4. Audience and persona development. Upload qualitative research transcripts, social listening exports, or focus group summaries. NotebookLM can cluster themes, extract verbatim evidence, and draft persona frameworks directly from what respondents actually said — rather than what a language model assumes they would say.

5. Content strategy from owned data. Load a client's existing blog archive, email campaigns, or social content calendar. Ask NotebookLM to identify coverage gaps, repeated themes, or underperforming topic clusters. This works particularly well for GCC brands that have produced Arabic and English content inconsistently — the tool can process both.

6. Audio Overviews for internal knowledge sharing. NotebookLM includes a feature that generates a conversational podcast-style audio summary of uploaded source material. For agency teams briefing account managers or creative leads who will not read a 40-page research document, this is a practical shortcut. It is imperfect, but it is faster than most alternatives.

7. Post-campaign analysis and reporting. Upload performance reports, client communications, and campaign briefs, then ask NotebookLM to draft a structured retrospective. Because it works only from the documents provided, the analysis reflects actual campaign data rather than a model's generalized view of what good performance looks like.

What to Be Realistic About

NotebookLM is not a general-purpose AI assistant, and trying to use it as one is frustrating. It requires well-structured, high-quality source documents to produce useful outputs — garbage in, garbage out applies here more strictly than with generative models. The free tier imposes notebook and source limits that production-volume agency use will hit quickly. And while Google has not published detailed enterprise data governance terms for the tool, agencies handling sensitive client data should review the current terms before uploading proprietary materials.

The Bigger Picture for GCC Agencies

The most capable agencies in the region over the next two years will not be those that used the most AI tools — they will be those that matched the right tool to the right workflow. NotebookLM is not a Swiss Army knife. It is closer to a high-precision instrument for structured research and synthesis. That specificity is a reason to use it, not to dismiss it. For teams in Riyadh, Dubai, or Cairo still running competitive research on search engines and slide decks, the upgrade is available now and costs nothing to test.
