OpenAI Is Building an App Store Alternative Inside ChatGPT — And GCC Marketing Teams Have a New Distribution Architecture to Build Into Every Growth and Acquisition Strategy Before the Platform Layer Sets

OpenAI is quietly repositioning ChatGPT as a discovery and execution layer for software, which means GCC growth teams may be engineering their acquisition funnels around a platform architecture that is already becoming obsolete.
September 30, 2026·6 min read·Conneqt

OpenAI is assembling the components of an alternative to the traditional app store model, according to reporting by TechCrunch AI. The direction is clear: ChatGPT is being developed into a surface where software can be discovered and used — not just by human users, but by AI agents acting on their behalf. If that architecture matures, it does not merely add a new marketing channel. It restructures who controls discoverability, what friction looks like, and which intermediaries extract value from every user acquisition event.

What Is Actually Being Built

The TechCrunch report frames OpenAI's moves as deliberately targeting the app store model — the distribution layer that Apple and Google have controlled for the better part of two decades. The mechanism is ChatGPT itself becoming the interface through which users find and execute software functions, without navigating to an app store, downloading an application, or even visiting a website. The implication is a fundamental reordering of the discovery funnel: intent is expressed inside ChatGPT, and fulfillment happens there too, mediated by AI rather than by a curated storefront or a search results page.

Critically, this is not limited to human users. The reporting specifically notes that both people and AI agents can discover and use software through this emerging layer. That distinction matters enormously for anyone thinking about growth architecture. An AI agent completing a task on a user's behalf does not browse an App Store, does not respond to a push notification, and does not convert through a retargeting ad. It queries, selects, and executes — and the selection criteria are determined by the model, not by the marketing budget behind a listing.

Why GCC Growth Teams Should Be Uncomfortable Right Now

Most digital acquisition strategies in the GCC are currently built on two foundational assumptions: that users discover products through search and social, and that conversion happens through owned properties — apps, websites, storefronts. Significant budget is allocated to App Store Optimization, paid app install campaigns, and search engine visibility. These assumptions are reasonable today. They may not be reasonable in three years.

The implication for MENA operators is not that ChatGPT will replace the App Store imminently — it will not, and the regulatory and commercial obstacles OpenAI faces are substantial. Apple and Google will not cede distribution economics without a fight, and their platform relationships with developers are backed by contractual architecture that will take years to unwind. The implication is narrower but more urgent: the window to experiment with and build positioning inside emerging AI-native discovery surfaces is open now, before the platform layer sets and before incumbency advantages accrue to early movers.

For GCC e-commerce, fintech, and on-demand services — sectors that have driven the majority of app install spend across markets like Saudi Arabia and the UAE — the specific risk is that AI-mediated discovery rewards legibility to a model rather than visibility to a human browser. A brand that has invested heavily in visual creative, influencer presence, and app store screenshots has built assets optimized for human perception. Those assets transfer poorly to an environment where an AI agent is evaluating which tool to invoke based on structured capability descriptions, reviews parsed as data, and prior usage signals.

What a Rational Response Looks Like

While the TechCrunch source focuses on OpenAI's product moves, the same pattern of AI-native discovery is visible across the competitive landscape, and the strategic response is not platform-specific. GCC growth teams should treat this as an architecture question, not a channel question. That means auditing how products are described and represented in machine-readable contexts — API documentation, structured data, integration directories — not just in consumer-facing creative. It means building relationships with the developer ecosystems that will wire products into agentic workflows. And it means allocating at least a portion of experimentation budget to understanding how AI systems currently represent and recommend products in their category.

None of that requires abandoning current acquisition infrastructure. It requires recognizing that the current infrastructure was built for a discovery paradigm that one of the most capitalized companies in technology is openly working to displace. In markets like KSA, where digital adoption curves are steep and platform lock-in happens fast, the teams that map the new architecture earliest will have the most leverage when it solidifies. The ones that wait for proof of scale may find the rent-seeking has already been baked in.
