OpenAI's Business Profiles Turn ChatGPT Into a Discovery Engine — GCC Marketers Have a New AI-Native Brand Presence Layer to Build Before the Format Sets

OpenAI is rolling out Business Profiles that let brands shape how ChatGPT presents them to users, creating a new owned-presence layer that GCC marketers should treat as seriously as they once treated Google My Business.
November 11, 2026·5 min read·Conneqt

OpenAI has begun rolling out Business Profiles for ChatGPT — a feature set designed to let companies assert more control over how they appear inside the model's responses. The move, reported by TechCrunch, is a deliberate push to position ChatGPT as a consumer discovery engine, not just a question-answering tool. For GCC marketing and growth teams, the timing matters: formats like this tend to reward early movers disproportionately, and the window before best practices harden is short.

What Business Profiles Actually Are

According to TechCrunch's reporting, OpenAI is introducing features that allow businesses to populate structured information about themselves — the kind of owned-presence mechanics that, in a previous era, defined the value of a Google Business Profile listing. The explicit framing from OpenAI is about making ChatGPT "a more robust consumer discovery engine," and the feature set is designed to help businesses contribute directly to that discovery layer.

The analogy to Google is not incidental — TechCrunch's headline makes it explicit. What OpenAI is building resembles the early architecture of local and brand search: a structured data layer that the model can surface when users ask conversational queries with commercial intent. Think "where should I open a business bank account in Riyadh" or "which dermatology clinic in Dubai has weekend appointments." These are queries that already happen inside ChatGPT. Business Profiles are OpenAI's mechanism for letting brands influence what comes back.

Why This Is an SEO-Adjacent Problem, Not a Social Media One

GCC marketing teams should resist the instinct to route this through their social or content functions. Business Profiles in an LLM context are closer in nature to technical SEO and structured data markup than to brand content. The underlying question is: what signals does the model have available when it decides whether and how to surface your brand? Owning a profile is the first step toward answering that question with something other than whatever the model scraped from third-party review sites or news articles.

This distinction matters in the GCC context specifically. A significant share of regional brand discovery still runs through word-of-mouth, WhatsApp recommendations, and mall foot traffic — channels where brands have little structured data presence at all. As AI-mediated discovery grows among the region's young, mobile-first population, the gap between brands that have structured their AI-facing presence and those that have not will compound quietly and quickly.

The Early-Mover Dynamic Is Real, But So Is the Uncertainty

It is worth being clear about what is not yet known. TechCrunch's reporting does not detail the ranking or weighting mechanics — meaning it is not established how much a completed Business Profile actually shifts a brand's prominence in ChatGPT responses, relative to other signals like web presence, citation frequency, or user feedback loops. Brands should not treat profile completion as a guaranteed traffic lever on the order of paid search.

What is more defensible is the structural argument: in every previous discovery platform — Google, Apple Maps, Tripadvisor — brands that established clean, accurate, complete structured presences early captured default positioning that proved durable. The implication for MENA operators is that the cost of acting now is low and the option value is real, even if the magnitude of return is not yet quantifiable.

What GCC Brands Should Actually Do

For growth and acquisition teams in the region, the practical near-term agenda is straightforward. First, treat Business Profiles as a new owned channel requiring the same accuracy discipline as a Google listing — wrong operating hours or a missing location create compounding downstream errors inside AI responses. Second, audit what ChatGPT currently says about your brand without a profile, since that baseline is the counterfactual you are trying to improve on. Third, involve SEO and content strategy teams — not just social — because the content signals that influence LLM outputs are closer to web authority mechanics than to engagement metrics.

The broader pattern is one that GCC digital marketers have navigated before: a dominant platform restructures how consumers discover businesses, rewards early structural investment, and then locks in defaults that latecomers spend years trying to unwind. ChatGPT's user growth makes it large enough to take seriously. Business Profiles are OpenAI's first formal invitation for brands to participate in shaping their own presence inside it. The invitation should not sit unanswered for long.
