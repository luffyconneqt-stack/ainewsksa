Google Just Released Real Data on How AI Mode Is Changing Search — And MENA Marketers Are Still Building for the Old Behaviour

Google's I/O redesign of the search box — its first in 25 years — comes with first-party usage data from AI Mode that should be forcing an immediate audit of keyword-centric content strategies across GCC markets.
June 2, 2026·6 min read·Conneqt

For twenty-five years, the Google search box was functionally unchanged: a white rectangle, a blinking cursor, a few typed keywords, and a ranked list of blue links. That interface shaped an entire industry. SEO, paid search, content marketing, and conversion funnels were all architected around the assumption that users would express intent in short, fragmented queries — and that Google would return a list of destinations for them to navigate. At Google I/O this week, that assumption was formally retired.

What Google Actually Announced

According to VentureBeat's reporting on the I/O announcements, Google has redesigned the search input itself for the first time in a quarter century. The text field is no longer a simple keyword box. It now accepts text, images, PDFs, videos, and open Chrome tabs as simultaneous inputs — a multimodal shift that changes the nature of what a "query" even is. Alongside this, Google is merging its AI Overviews and AI Mode into a single, unified search flow, removing the friction that previously required users to actively opt into AI-generated responses. The default experience is now conversational.

The significance of the merger between AI Overviews and AI Mode is structural, not cosmetic. Previously, a user had to make a deliberate choice to engage with the AI layer. That friction was, in effect, a buffer protecting traditional search behaviour — and traditional SEO logic. Eliminating that buffer means the conversational interface is no longer an alternative path; it is the path.

The Behavioural Shift Is No Longer Theoretical

What makes this moment materially different from previous AI search announcements is the availability of first-party usage data. Google is now publishing data on how users actually behave inside AI Mode — and the reported pattern is a measurable shift away from keyword fragments toward natural-language, multi-turn conversations. Users are not typing "best mortgage rates Saudi Arabia." They are asking follow-up questions, refining answers, and engaging in exchanges that look nothing like the query patterns that underpin current keyword research databases.

For MENA content and SEO teams, this is not a forecast. It is live performance data from the world's dominant search platform, one year after AI Mode launched in the US. The implication is direct: the keyword-volume models, the search-intent taxonomies, and the on-page optimisation frameworks that most regional teams built their 2024 strategies around were calibrated to a search behaviour that is actively contracting as a share of total queries.

Why GCC Markets Face a Compressed Timeline

There is a pattern in how AI search features have rolled out globally: the US serves as the data-generating environment, and markets in MENA typically receive the feature set six to eighteen months later — often with less warning and less preparation time. By the time AI Mode and the redesigned search experience reach Arabic-language queries at scale, the behavioural shift will already be mature in the platform's training data and ranking logic. GCC operators who wait for local rollout to begin adapting are, by definition, late.

The multimodal input capability compounds this. If Google's search box can now process PDFs, images, and open browser tabs, then the content asset — not just the keyword — becomes the unit of search relevance. A product PDF, a specification sheet, a comparison table: these are now potential query inputs, not just downloadable resources. Content teams that have never thought about how their PDFs are structured for machine parsing will need to.

What an Immediate Audit Should Cover

The analytical priority for MENA marketing and growth teams is not to predict where AI search is going — it is to audit where current infrastructure is misaligned with where it already is. That means examining whether content is written to answer discrete, conversational questions or to rank for isolated keyword strings. It means reviewing whether paid search assumptions about query intent still hold when the query is no longer a keyword but a conversation turn. And it means assessing whether content assets beyond web pages — PDFs, video, structured data — are machine-readable in ways that a multimodal search index can use.

Google has now published the data and shipped the interface. The behavioural shift is not coming. For US users, it has already arrived. For MENA operators, the preparation window is narrowing, and the cost of optimising for a search paradigm that Google has formally retired will compound with every quarter it goes unaddressed.
