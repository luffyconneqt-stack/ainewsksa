Blockchain ad networks in 2026: a buyer's guide for marketers

Bitmedia, Coinzilla, A-ADS, Persona, Quex, Brave. Six networks, six audiences, six pricing models. A practical guide for marketers running spend in crypto-native channels.
May 6, 2026·7 min read·Conneqt

Crypto-native ad networks are no longer the workaround they were in 2021. They're now the primary acquisition channel for any crypto project, and an increasingly viable option for non-crypto operators in restricted verticals. Six networks dominate the space, and each has a distinct audience, pricing model, and acceptance posture. Knowing which to use for what is the difference between a campaign that hits ROAS and one that burns budget on the wrong inventory.

Bitmedia

Type: Display + native. Audience: Global crypto-aware consumers. CPM range: $1–4. Best for: Performance campaigns at scale where the funnel is well-tuned and creative volume is the bottleneck.

Bitmedia is the largest crypto display network and the closest analog to a traditional DSP for crypto inventory. EU and US reach are strong; APAC less so. Acceptance is relatively open for compliant operators. The platform rewards creative variant volume — campaigns running 30+ variants typically outperform ones running 5–10 by 2× on ROAS.

Coinzilla

Type: Display + native + press distribution. Audience: Premium crypto media readers (Cointelegraph, CryptoSlate, et al.). CPM range: $2–6. Best for: Brand campaigns and product launches where placement quality matters more than raw scale.

Coinzilla's edge is the press release distribution network — paid coverage in crypto media tier-1s. For projects that need credibility signals, this is often more valuable than the display inventory itself. CPMs run higher than Bitmedia; the audience is more engaged.

A-ADS

Type: Display. Audience: Crypto-only, broad. CPM range: $0.5–2. Best for: Anonymous campaigns, gray-market verticals, or test budgets where bottom-funnel quality matters less than impression volume.

A-ADS is the network of last resort for many advertisers — anonymous, no KYC for advertisers, accepts BTC and other crypto for ad spend. The inventory quality is lower; click fraud and bot traffic are higher. Used judiciously for impression building or top-funnel awareness in restricted verticals, it can hit ROAS. Used as a primary channel, it usually doesn't.

Persona

Type: Programmatic. Audience: Web3-native, on-chain attribution. CPM range: Custom (auction-based). Best for: Targeting by wallet behavior, holder demographics, or on-chain activity patterns.

Persona is the most sophisticated targeting platform in the space. Advertisers can segment by held tokens, transaction history, wallet age, DEX activity, and dozens of other on-chain signals. It's overkill for most projects but indispensable for protocols targeting specific holder segments (DeFi power users, NFT collectors, specific token communities).

Quex

Type: Exchange and DeFi inventory. Audience: Active traders. CPM range: $3–8. Best for: High-intent inventory aimed at users actively trading or using DeFi protocols.

Quex specializes in inventory placed on crypto exchange interfaces and DeFi dApps — high-intent placements where users are already in financial-action mode. CPMs are premium; conversion rates often justify them for trading-related products (other exchanges, DeFi protocols, derivatives platforms).

Brave Ads

Type: Privacy-first. Audience: Brave browser users. CPM range: $5–15. Best for: High-trust audiences, privacy-aligned products, longer-form messaging.

Brave Ads is opt-in — users get paid in BAT tokens to view advertising. The audience is small but engaged; the trust profile is unique because users have actively chosen to see ads. CPMs are highest in the category, but click-through rates often justify the premium for products with privacy or crypto-native positioning.

Cross-network strategy

Mature crypto projects don't pick one network. They run a portfolio with each network handling a different funnel stage. Brave for trust-oriented brand work. Coinzilla for credibility-driving press placements. Persona for targeted holder acquisition. Bitmedia for scaled performance. A-ADS for impression top-up in markets where other networks have limits. Quex for high-intent commerce.

The bottleneck across all of them is creative volume. Each network has different specs, copy length limits, and audience-appropriate hooks. Real ROAS optimization runs 30–50 variants per campaign per network. Manual creative production caps spend; AI-driven creative pipelines have started to remove that ceiling.
