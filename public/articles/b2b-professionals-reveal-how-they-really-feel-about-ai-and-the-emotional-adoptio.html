B2B Professionals Reveal How They Really Feel About AI — And the Emotional Adoption Gap GCC Marketing Leaders Are Failing to Measure

New SmarterX research surfaces not just where B2B teams stand in their AI journey but how they feel about it — a distinction that exposes a blind spot in how GCC marketing leaders measure and manage adoption.
June 13, 2026·6 min read·Conneqt

Most AI adoption metrics in enterprise settings measure activity: tools deployed, prompts run, hours saved. What they rarely capture is sentiment — whether the people using those tools feel confident, anxious, resigned, or genuinely transformed. New research from SmarterX, surfaced by the Marketing AI Institute, attempts to close that gap by surveying B2B professionals not just on where they are in their AI journey, but on how they feel about it. The distinction is more consequential than it might appear.

What the Research Actually Measures

The SmarterX study, presented at a B2B marketers summit and reported by the Marketing AI Institute, examines the emotional and attitudinal dimensions of AI adoption among B2B professionals. Rather than treating adoption as a binary — either teams use AI or they don't — the research maps a more granular landscape in which usage and genuine buy-in can diverge significantly. A professional can be logging into an AI tool daily while privately doubting its value, deferring to its outputs without trusting them, or complying with a mandate they did not choose. These are meaningfully different states, and they produce meaningfully different outcomes.

The source material does not break down specific sentiment percentages or name the exact attitudinal categories the research employs. What it does establish is that the emotional dimension of the AI journey is both measurable and distinct from behavioral metrics — and that surfacing it changes the picture of where teams actually stand.

Why Surface-Level Compliance Is a Real Problem

For GCC marketing leaders, the compliance-versus-conviction gap is not an abstract concern. Across the region, AI adoption has in many cases been driven top-down: leadership announces a strategic commitment to AI, vendors are onboarded, and usage targets get set. What this approach systematically underweights is the attitudinal soil in which genuine transformation either takes root or quietly fails to grow.

The implication of the SmarterX framing is that an organization can hit its AI usage KPIs while its teams remain emotionally disengaged — producing outputs that look like AI-assisted work but lack the iterative trust-building and workflow integration that compound over time into real capability. This is not a technology problem. It is a change management and diagnostic problem, and it requires different instrumentation than most marketing operations teams currently deploy.

Diagnosing the Emotional Gap

While the source focuses on the B2B professional population broadly, the same diagnostic logic applies with particular force in MENA markets where AI literacy varies sharply across seniority levels, functions, and language backgrounds. A senior Arabic-speaking strategist in Riyadh and a junior English-trained digital analyst in Dubai may both be listed as "active AI users" in a platform dashboard while sitting in entirely different emotional relationships with the technology. One may feel augmented; the other may feel surveilled or replaceable. Neither sentiment is visible in a usage log.

The practical framework that emerges from taking the SmarterX research seriously involves asking different questions at the team level: not just "are people using the tools" but "do they believe the tools are making their work better," "do they feel equipped to push back when outputs are wrong," and "do they see AI as something happening to them or something they are actively directing." These questions are not soft. They are leading indicators of whether an AI investment will compound or plateau.

What GCC Marketing Leaders Should Do Differently

For GCC marketing and growth leaders trying to drive genuine transformation rather than reportable compliance, the research points toward a few concrete adjustments. First, add attitudinal measurement to whatever adoption metrics are already in place — even informal pulse surveys that capture confidence and perceived usefulness will surface signal that usage data misses. Second, treat emotional resistance as diagnostic information rather than a communication failure to be overcome with more training. Resistance often maps onto legitimate gaps in tool quality, workflow fit, or role clarity. Third, distinguish between teams that are experimenting with AI and teams that have genuinely restructured how they work around it — the gap between those two states is where most enterprise AI investments currently stall.

The SmarterX research does not claim to have solved AI adoption. What it does is establish that measuring adoption without measuring sentiment is measuring the wrong thing — and that the organizations that close this gap first will have a compounding advantage over those still counting logins.
