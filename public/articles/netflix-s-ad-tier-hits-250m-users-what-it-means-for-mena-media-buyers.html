Netflix's Ad Tier Hits 250M Users: What It Means for MENA Media Buyers

Netflix's ad-supported plan now reaches 250 million users globally and is expanding into vertical video and podcasts, forcing GCC media buyers to reassess how they allocate connected TV and streaming budgets.
May 14, 2026·7 min read·Conneqt

For the second consecutive year, Netflix's ad-supported tier more than doubled its monthly user base. At its 2026 upfront presentation, the company disclosed that its $8.99-per-month plan now reaches 250 million subscribers globally — up from 94 million reported just a year earlier. The platform also reported $1.5 billion in advertising revenue for 2025. These are not incremental gains. They represent a structural reorientation of one of the world's most-watched content platforms, and they carry direct consequences for media buyers operating across the GCC and broader MENA region.

The Inventory Expansion That Changes the Equation

The scale milestone matters, but the more operationally significant announcement is where Netflix intends to place ads next. Starting in 2026, the platform will introduce advertising into two new surfaces: the vertical video feed it recently launched on its mobile app, and the podcast lineup it began building out in late 2024. Both moves are notable for different reasons. The vertical video feed directly mirrors the format that has driven advertiser spending on TikTok and Instagram Reels — short, scroll-driven, full-screen placements that command higher attention metrics than mid-roll or banner inventory. The podcast expansion, meanwhile, opens an audio-first channel that carries lower CPMs but strong completion rates and listener engagement. Together, they signal that Netflix is no longer competing solely in the linear TV replacement market. It is positioning itself as a full-funnel media environment.

Netflix launched its advertising business in late 2022 in partnership with Microsoft's Xandr for ad tech infrastructure, though it has since been building out its own in-house ad platform. The company has been progressively loosening its original posture of being ad-light and premium-only. That evolution is now accelerating.

Why MENA Media Buyers Are Watching Closely

Across the GCC, connected TV adoption has grown faster than most Western markets anticipated. Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Kuwait consistently rank among the highest globally for per-capita streaming consumption. Netflix penetration in KSA and the UAE is substantial, though the company does not break out regional subscriber figures. What matters for regional media buyers is that a meaningful share of those 250 million ad-tier users is sitting in their target markets — and until now, they have had limited programmatic access to that audience at scale.

MENA media buyers have historically faced a structural gap in the connected TV space. Google's DV360 and The Trade Desk offer CTV inventory through various partnerships, but premium streaming inventory — particularly on Netflix — has been tightly controlled and largely accessible only through direct upfront commitments weighted toward large global advertisers. The expansion of Netflix's ad surfaces, and particularly the move toward more programmatic access that the company has signaled over the past 18 months, could begin to change that calculus for regional buyers working with mid-range budgets.

The Format Risk Regional Buyers Should Not Ignore

The vertical video expansion deserves scrutiny, not just optimism. Netflix's mobile feed is a new and largely unproven environment. The platform does not yet have the algorithmic content recommendation depth of TikTok or the creator ecosystem of YouTube Shorts, both of which give advertisers context signals that improve targeting relevance. Ads appearing in a vertical feed that users are still learning to engage with — or may abandon — carry higher execution risk than mid-roll placements within known, high-completion content like a drama series or a stand-up special. Buyers should treat this inventory category as experimental in its first cycle and budget accordingly, rather than shifting significant allocations based on format familiarity alone.

Podcast inventory presents a different risk profile. Netflix's podcast catalog is still nascent compared to Spotify or Apple Podcasts, and Arabic-language podcast content on the platform remains sparse. For KSA and UAE buyers targeting Arabic-speaking audiences specifically, this channel's utility in the near term is limited.

What to Watch Before the Next Budget Cycle

The meaningful question for MENA operators is not whether Netflix's ad tier is growing — it clearly is — but whether the platform will provide regional buyers with the targeting granularity, measurement transparency, and budget flexibility to justify reallocation from existing channels. Netflix has not yet demonstrated parity with Google or Meta on attribution tooling in MENA markets. Until it does, its role in a regional media mix should be evaluated as a reach and brand-building vehicle with premium context, not as a performance channel. The 250 million user figure is a ceiling worth planning around. The infrastructure to activate it efficiently in this region is still being built.
